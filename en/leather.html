Fit

Ease is not only softness.

A sneaker wraps the foot in cushion. That is the soft feeling of the first day.

A leather shoe that fits is held by the last. The leather follows the bumps of that person’s foot. It can feel firm at first. A pair that fits can leave the foot easier at the end of a long day.

A leather shoe that does not fit hurts more than a sneaker. The number on the size is not enough. Try the last on your foot.

This is not a medical claim. It is what we have seen, fitting shoes and repairing them. Sneakers are not the problem. Cushion and support are two kinds of ease.

  • 01 Last

    A last carved from Japanese feet sets where the foot sits. RENDO’s lasts are made in Asakusa.

  • 02 Wear

    Leather learns the foot inside it. Cloth and foam do not follow that far.

  • 03 Try them

    We will not push a pair that does not fit. Decide after you wear it in the studio.

Repair

When the sole wears, replace the sole.

Most RENDO shoes are Goodyear welted. A strip of leather, the welt, is stitched between the upper and the sole, and the sole is stitched to that. When the sole or heel wears, the upper stays and the worn part is replaced.

On many sneakers the sole and the upper are glued into one piece. MOGGYS repairs sneakers too. The structure is still different from a shoe built so the sole can be changed.

After wearing, let the shoe rest. Wipe off the dirt. Cream only when the leather needs it. Rotating two pairs lets the leather recover more than wearing one pair every day.

Only the Grecian slipper is McKay construction. It is not made so a welt stays while the sole is replaced. The other seven are the shoes you repair and keep wearing.

Eight shapes

The eight shapes do different work.

Formal days, shoes that come off, travel, days off, summer. These eight are the ready-made and made-to-order RENDO shoes. The roots are the history of the shapes. The specifications are today’s RENDO. A pattern order, from your own last, is a different commission. See what that includes

26S770C01

Cap-toe oxford

Suits, ceremonies, interviews

The oxford spread in the nineteenth century at Oxford as a shoe lower than a boot, with closed lacing. A cap toe adds a separate piece of leather over the toe. With the punching kept quiet, it became the city dress shoe. The holes themselves come from country shoes, cut to let water out of wet ground.

This RENDO shoe rebuilds the first model, no.7701, after twelve years. A firmer leather and a thick toe puff help the shape stay. If you keep one leather pair, start by trying this.

7705

Double monk

A suit, with a different face from an oxford

The name comes from monk. It is a dress shoe closed with a buckle, without laces. A double monk uses two straps. The occasion is a little softer than an oxford.

RENDO uses two narrow straps and small buckles. The overlap on the instep stays clean.

Specifications and price

7703

Side elastic

Days when the shoes come off often

Elastic in the sides is known from the boot J. Sparkes-Hall made in London in 1851 for Queen Victoria. RENDO moves that idea onto a closed-lacing low shoe.

There are no laces. Elastic on both sides makes it easier on days you take the shoes off often. It still reads as a dress shoe.

Specifications and price

R7706D

Chukka boot

A jacket and trousers, days with a lot of walking

Chukka takes its name from one period of polo. It is an ankle boot. In the middle of the twentieth century, the desert boot, based on military boots in Cairo, brought that height into everyday wear.

RENDO is Goodyear welted, on a Dainite sole. There is smooth leather, and Charles F. Stead suede. Suede suits the colder months.

Specifications and price

26S270H01

Loafer

Jacket days, mornings without laces

The loafer traveled from Norwegian hand-sewn shoes to America, and spread as the G.H. Bass Weejun in 1936. Students slipping a coin into the strap is where the name penny loafer comes from.

RENDO revisits no.2701 on the slip-on last, 270. Because laces cannot adjust it, the throat was tightened. If your shoes come off often, try this beside the cap toe.

Specifications and price

26S270P002GS Made to order

Grecian slipper

Evening, and clothes close to indoor wear

The Grecian slipper is a low-cut slip-on worn indoors and in the evening in the nineteenth century. It uses little leather, so the shape of the foot shows.

RENDO makes it from two pieces of leather, with lizard nubuck at the collar. The construction is McKay. It is made to order, and the lead time is about three to four months. It does not stand in for formal dress.

Specifications and price

GB001

Apron derby

Denim, knitwear, days off

A derby has open lacing. The instep opens more than an oxford, so the foot goes in more easily and the fit can be adjusted. It was a shoe for outdoors, rather than a city oxford. An apron derby sets a U-shaped piece over the toe, close to the face of a Norwegian hand-sewn shoe.

RENDO’s last is GB, separate from the dress last 770. The sole is Ridgeway. It suits days off and neat everyday clothes. It does not stand in for a formal suit.

Specifications and price

26SGBFG Made to order

Punched summer shoe

A jacket on a hot day

Brogue punching began in the wet ground of Scotland and Ireland, to let water out of the shoe. On a summer shoe, the same holes let air through.

RENDO punches the body and uses Adelaide facings. It is an unlined Goodyear welt, descended from the 2019 FG. The last is the casual GB. The holes mean it is not for ceremonies or formal days.
